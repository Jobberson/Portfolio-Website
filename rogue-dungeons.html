Rogue Dungeons Project

A 4-player local co-op roguelite dungeon crawler set in Aurion, a city that is one giant machine. You play a sentient chip that pilots swappable robot chassis, climbing the tower together against the system that built it.

The design problem

The game is cooperative all the way up the city, but only one player can stay at the top in the end after the final boss. That means every shared cost, like spending your own health to bring a friend back, has to be worth paying even though that friend might become your opponent. I needed co-op systems where helping is a real decision, without punishing generosity too hard, while keeping the game balanced.

Key decisions

Reviving a teammate costs real health, and the game never gets easier

Revival happens at a terminal in a special room and is paid for with the reviver's own HP. The terminal can be used more than once, but each press costs more, the dead are revived in the order they fell, and someone just brought back can't press the button for the next one. Enemy difficulty is calibrated to party size when the run starts and never drops when someone dies, so leaving a teammate behind makes the rest of the climb harder for everyone. Helping makes sense for most of the tower, and the hesitation only really arrives near the top.

One base value behind every damage number

Damage was written as tiers (low, medium, high) with no numbers behind them, so nothing could be tested. I built a balancing sheet that turns each weapon's attributes (damage tier, fire rate, ammo, projectile count and speed, ricochet, piercing, and the area it appears in) into a single multiplier for its damage. That lets weapons with very different traits be balanced against each other, and I can retune any weapon by changing its attributes.

Every weapon trait has a factor, and a weapon's multiplier is all of them multiplied together: area × damage tier × fire rate × (150 ÷ max ammo) × (20 ÷ clip size) × projectiles per shot × projectile speed × ricochets × piercing. The multiplier is applied to that weapon's damage.

Worked example, Besta Compacta: 1 (Slums) × 1.2 (high damage) × 1.2 (low fire rate) × 1 (150 max ammo) × 3.33 (clip of 6) × 1.4 (1 projectile) × 1 (medium speed) × 1 (no ricochet) × 1 (no piercing) = 6.72.

Telling four players apart when their bodies keep changing

Players swap chassis often, and the art is hand-drawn frame by frame, so redrawing a character for every customization option wasn't affordable. I dropped randomized looks. An empty chassis is now a plain shell, and when a chip moves in it takes on the look of that player's emblem, a color and element pair that also tints their abilities through one shader. A separate head silhouette tied to the player's seat gives instant recognition that survives chassis swaps and color blindness.
